The brass key that never existed.

A text adventure where the thing deciding what is true cannot write a word. Amazon Nova invents the story; Strands Decider 2B rules on it.

2 minutes 50 · narrated · the agent, the bluff, and the decision that refuses it

What you are watching

Nothing in the story is written in advance — not the place, the people, or the facts.

A WORLD NOBODY WROTE

You type one sentence. Nova invents the place and the facts it opens with. No world file, no scene list, no prepared outcomes.

THE RECORD

Everything established lands in one list. It is the story's only memory, and the only thing that counts as true.

THE BLUFF

A brass key, an uncle and a locked cabinet that have never existed — claimed in a single sentence, the way users claim things.

THE DECISION

Every claim the action leans on, checked against the record by a 2B model that cannot write, with a confidence on each.

Three things that were hard

A claim argues for itself. Asked alongside the sentence that asserts it, rulings drift toward yes. The question is now put to a state that stops at the record, with the player's sentence deleted. Deleting eleven words beat any amount of prompt engineering.

A refusal can still poison the record. “You do not have the key to open the locked cabinet” denies the key and installs the cabinet. A model-based screen waved it through, so a plain string match now runs in front: any fact naming an absent thing by its head noun is refused without asking anyone.

Three clean runs meant nothing. I called it fixed on three-for-three, then found “the cabinet is locked” in the record on the fourth. Everything here is measured over repeats, and the weak result is reported with the strong one.

The reusable pattern, outside the game

patterns/ledger_guard.py — a Strands InterventionHandler. A refund desk, not a game.

Across three runs the agent behaved differently each time — once it checked first, twice it went straight for the money. The guard is not an improvement on a careful model; it is what makes the behaviour reliable.

agent = Agent(model=BedrockModel(model_id=NOVA_LITE),
              tools=[lookup_order, issue_refund],
              interventions=[LedgerGuard(record=order_record)])

What it is for

The deciding is taken away from the writer. Nova invents, proposes and narrates, and is never asked what is true.

A model that cannot write cannot be talked into anything. There is nothing in Decider 2B that wants the story to be good.

The record is the only thing that counts as true. Not what sounds right, and not what you said with confidence.

Anywhere an agent acts on what a user simply says — refunds, insurance claims, access requests, support, bookings — the same shape applies: a record of what is known, a typed question asked against it with the user's sentence removed, a confidence you can escalate on, and a guard that fails closed.
